MY LEARNING · LESSON 1

Why do you want to study in the United States?

Build an answer from your own academic interests and plans.

Not completed

1. Understand the question

Use this question to explain your purpose for studying abroad. Begin with what you want to learn and why it matters to you. You do not need a dramatic story or a memorized speech.

Think about three connections:

  1. Your subject: What interests you, and what do you want to understand or be able to do?
  2. Your study opportunity: What specific part of your chosen U.S. program supports that interest? Check the university's own information before making a claim.
  3. Your plans: How could that learning help with your genuine goals? If your plans are still developing, say so.
Make a broad reason specific

“The education is good” does not explain your own choice. Ask yourself: which learning opportunity matters to me, and why?

These are thinking prompts, not a script. Use only details you know are true.

Before you practise

You may need to research your course first. It is fine to write “I still need to check” in your notes. The tutor can help you identify what is missing.

This lesson teaches clear communication. Completing it does not indicate visa eligibility or guarantee an outcome.

2. Find your own reasons

These study-purpose notes are optional before speaking. Your tutor also uses your saved student profile.

3. Learn with your tutor

Get short explanations tailored to your profile: study purpose, school choice, funding, and future plans. Ask a question, practise if you want, or say “next” to move on.
